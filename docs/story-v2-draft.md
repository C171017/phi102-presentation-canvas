# PHI 102 canvas · Story draft 2: *Eight-ish*

**Draft for review, November 21, 2026.** This revises the plot in [story-and-characters.md](story-and-characters.md) by adding a cold-open hook, plants and payoffs, a red herring and a twist. The cast, designs, setting and canvas order stay the same. Each character's temperament, as defined in the [character bible](character-bible.md), now *drives* a plot turn, so the story and the characters stay coherent. Alternative versions are at the end for comparison.

## Logline

Simone races the last train to get a hermit crab to his surprise party by eight. Then she finds out the real danger was never being late.

## The engine in one paragraph

Simone believes, with good justification, that the party starts at **8:00**. Every context on the way there (the group chat, the platform, the train) lets that belief stand as a foundation. Only Rae, whose context doesn't take Evan's handwriting for granted, rules it NOT VALID, and she explains nothing. At Evan's door the card is fully unfolded for the first time. The blue mark across the fold is a mirror-image **":30"**, and this is a *surprise* party, so the guest of honour has to arrive **after** the guests. Arriving early would ruin it. What Evan wrote can no longer fix what Simone should believe, because she can't read it. Her grounds have to move to something she *can* know: Cole's stream archive of Evan's past parties, the mirror image, her best guess. She works out what she should believe, and at 7:59 she lowers her hand and doesn't knock. She was justified, she was wrong, and she can still tell what she should believe. That is the whole of Greco's paper in one evening.

## The stream audience is the objector-group

Greco's first route to flexible foundations starts from the idea that justifying is something we do *for an audience*. "Who that audience is – and more importantly, which claims they'll let pass without objection – will vary from context to context" (§2.1, following Annis's *objector-group*). Cole is a livestreamer, so he always has an audience. The story makes his stream chat the objector-group.

**How it works on screen.** Cole's chat runs as an overlay. When he says a reason and the chat lets it pass (👍, no replies), the claim settles into place as a **foundation stone tinted rust**, because it is a foundation *for this audience*. When someone objects ("source??"), the claim can't end the chain: Cole has to give a further reason, and no stone forms. When the audience changes, which stones form changes too.

**Cole's three audiences**

| Audience | Who they are | What they let pass | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The chill chat** | Evan's group chat, watching together | "Evan wrote 8 👍" passes at once | An easygoing objector-group: the chain stops early |
| **The pedant chat** | The Timetable Society, three trainspotters standing on the platform and typing in his chat as @platform3purists | Nothing without a source: "Evan wrote 8" → "photo of the card?" → "how do you know it's his handwriting?" | A strict objector-group: different claims count as foundations |
| **The raid** | The Retrograde Car's channel floods in mid-stream | "Mercury retrograde = delays 🔮" passes with no objection | Greco's astrology worry: a whole audience can let a bad claim pass, and consensus doesn't make it foundational |

**How the audience shapes what Cole says.** Cole reads his chat before he commits to anything. With the chill chat, he says "it's at eight, done" and moves on. When the pedants flood in, he snaps into "for the record" mode and starts adding reasons. This is the bridge from the social route to contextualism. When Cole says "Simone is justified in taking Evan's word for granted," the audience he is addressing helps fix *what his sentence says*: roughly, "her belief would survive *this* group's objections." The words stay the same. The claim they express changes.

**Keep the four views distinct.** The chat's role differs by view, and the slides should not blur them:

| View | Does Cole's chat decide whether Simone's belief is foundational? |
| --- | --- |
| Social route, general (slide 9) | It shows the mechanism: audiences differ in which claims they let pass. |
| Sensitive invariantism (slide 13) | **No.** What matters is *Simone's own* community and inquiry. Cole's chat is his audience, not hers. |
| Contextualism (slides 15–16) | It helps fix *what Cole's sentence expresses*, not the facts about Simone. |
| Relativism (slide 17) | No. Rae assesses by her own standards, wherever the chat stands. |
| Expressivism | No. Echo's claw expresses a commitment and describes no audience. |

The chat also carries the story's main clue. "evan's parties never start on the hour lol" comes from a single chatter, and the rest of the chat scrolls past it. One lone objection isn't the objector-group, so the claim "Evan wrote 8 p.m." still passes. The ignored objection turns out to be the true one, which shows again that justification and truth come apart.

## The full story, beat by beat

**Q** marks a live quiz. Clip numbers refer to short generated video clips.

### Cold open (before the OP)

**7:59 p.m.** A dim hallway. A dark teal door with amber light under the sill. Simone's knuckles hover an inch from the wood. Echo watches from the carrier. Cole holds his breath on stream. Simone lowers her hand.

Title card: **Why didn't she knock?** Smash cut into the OP.

### OP

An homage to an anime opening: credits, flashed terms (*context*, *hinges*, *undoubted*, *E = K*, *access*), and story frames: Simone, the carrier, the crab, a clock at 6:41, the 7:12 departure board, a conductor's punch, a loop of blue ink. In the last frame, the invitation folds while the ink is still glistening. **"78 minutes earlier."**

## Quiz checkpoints

1. **Regress (slide 6).** Simone's chain of reasons ends at "Evan wrote it." Which response to the regress is that? *Foundationalism.*
2. **Hinges (slide 10).** Simone and Cole argue about morning versus evening. What are they holding fixed? *That the invite is genuine.*
3. **Whose context? (slide 12).** Match each view to the character it puts the ring on. Simone/SI, Cole/contextualism, Rae/relativism, and Echo/expressivism (no ring: a commitment is expressed).
4. **Disagreement (slide 17).** Why doesn't "I'm hungry / I'm not" conflict, when Cole's and Rae's justification lines seem to? *Each hunger claim is about its speaker; the justification claims seem to be about one belief.*
5. **Mobile home (slide 20).** The hour is unreadable. What should now fix what Simone believes about the start time? *Something she can still know: past parties, the mirrored mark, or her best guess.*

Optional, for Route 1: "When Cole is streaming to the Timetable Society, which claims can end his chain of reasons?" *Only those that group won't object to.* Optional, for SI: "Does Cole's chat decide whether Simone's belief is foundational on sensitive invariantism?" *No: her own community and inquiry do.*

Optional bonus at the cold-open payoff: "Was Simone justified in believing 8:00 on the train?" *Yes. Justified and wrong.*

## What changed from draft 1, and why

- **A cold open** gives the audience a question from second one, and slide 20 answers it.
- **The early-not-late twist** turns the smudge from a detail into a reversal. The train race was aimed at the wrong danger, and the access section becomes the climax instead of an epilogue.
- **Rae's NOT VALID becomes a clue.** Her silence is in character (she never explains) and makes the relativism slide memorable. The presenter note guards against reading it as "Rae was right."
- **The April Fools' prank becomes a red herring**, so the hinge scene also misdirects the audience.
- **Cole's stream chat is the objector-group.** The social route becomes something the audience watches happen: claims the chat lets pass turn into rust foundation stones, and changing audiences change which stones form. This links Route 1 to the contextualism scenes and plants the main clue.
- **Cole's archive and selfie camera supply the new evidence**, so his streaming habit pays off.
- **The surprise is staged twice**, which gives Rae her one crack and Echo his clack.

## Open choices

- **Mirror reading.** If the mirrored ":30" settles the question too neatly, keep the mark ambiguous ("could be :30, could be :00") so that past parties and her best guess do more of the work. That is closer to Greco's distributed confidence.
- **Who says "You're early"?** Evan, as written, or Rae at 8:30 (then her line is "Valid" *and* "You were early").
- **Timing.** The cold open and the double surprise add roughly 30–40 seconds. Trim the stranger or the weather presenter if needed.

# Alternative versions for comparison

Each alternative keeps the cast and their temperaments unless noted. They range from small changes to a completely different frame.

## A · Draft 1 plus (smallest change)

Keep draft 1's plot and add only the **cold open** and **Rae's lingering glance** at the invitation. The ending stays "8:30, you're early," without the surprise-party reversal.

- **Gains:** almost no new production. Existing frames 1–6 stay valid.
- **Loses:** no reversal. The access section remains an epilogue rather than a climax.

## B · *The Shell* (the metaphor made literal)

Same plot as draft 2, but the climax doubles as a visual metaphor. Greco calls the facts that fix one's justification a **cognitive home**, and argues that the home can be *local and mobile*. A hermit crab is literally an animal with a mobile home. Evan's birthday gift for Echo is a new shell. At slide 20, as Simone moves her grounds from "what Evan wrote" to "past parties and best guess," Echo climbs out of his old shell and into the new one *in the same shot*. He has a new home and the same crab, just as Simone has new grounds and the same belief-forming agent.

- **Gains:** the most memorable single image for the hardest concept in the talk, and it ties Echo to Part 3.
- **Risks:** it reopens the decision to keep Echo's Version C shell (the switch could happen only at the very end), and an expressivism mascot then carries an access metaphor too. The shell-switch frame already exists from earlier work.

## C · *It's Simone's party* (an emotional twist)

Same journey, different reveal: the party was never for Echo. It's for **Simone**, who has crab-sat for everyone for a year. Evan and Cole planned it together. Echo was the decoy, the stream audience is the guest list, and that's why the chat keeps hinting "never on the hour." Evan wrote 8:00 on Simone's card so she'd arrive *after* the 8:30 guests were hidden. (Swap the times as needed.) Cole's April Fools' joke is a real slip, nearly spoiling the secret.

- **Philosophy:** this matches Greco's own prankster case (§4.2): if Peter has a reputation as a prankster, what he said stops fixing Justine's justification. Simone was justified and was deliberately misled. Cole's stream hints become higher-order doubt.
- **Character payoff:** Simone's calm finally breaks into open warmth, a strong level-3 moment. Rae is a guest who knew the whole time, which fits her sealed character perfectly.
- **Risks:** it makes Evan a friendly deceiver, which complicates "Evan = evidence," and the surprise needs careful planting to feel fair.

## D · *Final Answer* (a completely different frame: a quiz show)

The whole talk is a late-night game show, *Are You Justified?* Cole hosts and switches register between the studio audience and the "home audience." Simone is the contestant whose prize is getting to Echo's party. Rae is the judge, who stamps every answer VALID or NOT VALID by her own standards. Echo is the buzzer, whose claw locks in an answer. Each live quiz is literally a round, and the class *is* the studio audience. The train story survives as filmed "contestant packages" between rounds.

- **Gains:** the live quizzes become the core of the show rather than interruptions. Rae's relativist judging and Cole's two audiences are built into the format.
- **Risks:** a new set and a new OP concept. Less room for a cinematic anime opening, and the existing train frames would become inserts rather than the spine.

## E · *Murder on the 7:12* (a different genre: a whodunit)

Echo's birthday cake vanishes from the train. Simone has to find out who took it before Eastside. Every passenger's alibi asks for another reason (the regress). The Timetable Society act as the jury that decides which testimony needs no further support (the objector-group). The Retrograde Car blames Mercury (consensus can be wrong). Everyone takes for granted that the cake existed (a hinge) until someone questions it. Rae and Cole disagree about whether Simone is justified in accusing anyone. In the end, the key evidence (a frosting smudge) is ambiguous, and Simone has to work out what she can know instead.

- **Gains:** the most suspense, and a natural fit for testimony and evidence.
- **Risks:** a mystery competes with the philosophy for attention, and the solution has to be tracked across 20 slides. It also departs furthest from the approved plot.

## F · *The 7:12 Loop* (a time loop)

Simone relives 6:41 to 7:12 again and again. Each loop she tries a different exit from the regress: she never leaves (scepticism), answers Milo forever (infinitism), reasons in a circle (coherentism), or stops at a foundation (foundationalism). Each failure resets the clock. The loop breaks only when she works out which claims she can take for granted *in this inquiry*. The class votes on which assumption to hold fixed, which uses the branching option the brief considered.

- **Gains:** a superb Part 1. The regress exits become lived consequences.
- **Risks:** repetition is costly in clips. Parts 2 and 3 don't loop naturally, and votes that change the story conflict with the single-story preference.
