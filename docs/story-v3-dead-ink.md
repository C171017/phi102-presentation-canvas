# PHI 102 canvas · Story draft 3: *Dead Ink* (a murder mystery across contexts)

> **Superseded by [draft 4, *Until It Dries*](story-v4-until-it-dries.md)** (November 21, 2026). Kept on file for reference.

**Concept draft for discussion, November 21, 2026.** This records Howie's new direction: a murder mystery in a magical world, where the investigators travel between parallel universes, one for each route × version pairing. The trivial but decisive clue is a *time*. Nothing here is final. Bracketed questions mark what we still need to decide. Draft 2 ([*Eight-ish*](story-v2-draft.md)) stays on file. Several of its pieces carry over: Cole's stream as the objector-group, the smudged hour, and Rae's silence.

## Logline

On the night of Echo's surprise party, Evan, the fountain pen who is hosting, is found lifeless beside the cake. His last written words trail off at **"8:"**. To find out who killed him, the cast ride a night train through every way foundations could work. The answer depends on a time nobody could read, and on an assumption nobody thought to question.

## The world

### Foundation stones

In this world, everyone has **foundation stones**: small glowing stones that orbit a person and stand for the claims they are taking for granted *right now*. The beliefs a stone supports float just above it, linked to it by a thread of light. When a claim comes into doubt, its stone dims, cracks and dissolves. When a new claim gets taken for granted, a new stone condenses. Stones are *undoubted, not undoubtable*: any of them can dissolve, but while it's there, nobody needs to give reasons for it.

**Who can see the stones** [proposal]: people can see other people's stones plainly, but only glimpse their own when they look straight at the belief a stone holds up. That small rule pays off in Part 3. Greco's access question, "can you tell what you're justified in believing?", becomes "can you see your own stones?" The audience always sees everyone's stones, because the presentation has already taught them what the stones mean.

### How stones form (the three routes)

| Route | How a stone forms in this world |
| --- | --- |
| **Social activity** (Annis) | A stone condenses when a claim passes your audience without objection. Cole's livestream chat is the clearest case: a 👍 forms a stone, and a "source??" stops it. |
| **Hinges** (Wittgenstein) | Stones appear *automatically* the moment you ask a question. Whatever the question presupposes has to be held fixed to ask it. You can't ask "When did Evan die?" without a stone reading **"Evan is dead."** |
| **Knowledge at the base** (E = K) | A stone mints from anything you know, such as something seen with your own eyes. |
| **Belief at the base** (Harman) | [Possibly what Howie meant by "self-justification"; to confirm.] In one universe, *every* belief instantly becomes a stone. Comic chaos: the investigators there accuse everyone at once. |

### Parallel universes (the four versions)

Each version is a universe with different **stone physics**. The investigators travel by **the Context Line**, a night train between universes. Rae is its conductor. Her punch stamps a ticket with a route and a version, and the train arrives in that universe at **7:12 p.m. on the same evening**. Every trip is a replay of the night under different rules, so travel works as both teleporting and time travel.

| Universe (version) | Stone physics | Lead investigator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sensitive invariantism** | Stones belong to the *subject*. Whether one forms depends on her own community, inquiry and stakes. | Simone |
| **Contextualism** | Stones attach to *words*. "Simone is justified" lights different stones depending on who the speaker is talking to. | Cole |
| **Relativism** | A stone looks solid or hollow depending on *who is looking*, and no viewer is privileged. | Rae |
| **Expressivism** | There are no stones at all, only raised claws. Calling a belief foundational *is* raising a claw. | Echo |

Each lead investigator's universe matches their temperament in the [character bible](character-bible.md). Simone quietly updates on her own situation. Cole performs to his audience. Rae rules by her own standards and never explains. Echo commits without describing anything.

## The case

**The victim.** Evan, the courteous fountain pen hosting Echo's party. Guests find him lying still on the writing desk at **8:40**, capped, beside the cake.

**The clue.** Under him lies the invitation he was writing. The hour trails off: **"8:"**, followed by a smear across the fold.

**Planted fact.** Earlier, Evan boasts, with his signature bow, that his ink dries in exactly ten minutes.

**Suspects** (each one is "justifiably" accused in some universe):
- **Cole:** his selfie stick swung over the desk on stream.
- **Rae:** her punch pliers could snap a nib, and she arrived "right after it happened."
- **Milo:** sticky hands, and he was asking Evan "why?" all evening.
- **The Timetable Society:** Evan wrote the wrong departure time in their minutes.
- **Echo:** a claw, and no alibi.

**The solution** [proposal]. **Nobody killed Evan, because Evan isn't dead. He ran out of ink.** The smear isn't a struggle: it's a pen running dry mid-stroke while writing **8:3**. Every investigation in every universe was built on one hinge stone, "Evan is dead," which formed automatically the moment anyone asked who killed him, and nobody examined it. Simone refills him. Evan revives, bows, and says: "You're early."

This keeps the story's spine: **justified, and wrong.** It turns Greco's hinges into the twist, and it stays dry and a little silly. [Alternatives with a real culprit are under Open questions.]

## Part 1 · The case opens (flexible foundationalism, the regress, the dilemma)

The job of Part 1 is to give the audience everything they need: the world, the stones, the regress and the dilemma.

1. **Cold open, 8:40.** A scream. Evan is motionless on the desk. Simone, calm: "Nobody touch the card." The smeared "8:" fills the screen.
2. **Contemporary contextualism.** Cole on stream: "Simone knows who did it." His chill chat believes it; his pedant chat demands proof. Same sentence, different claims.
3. **Flexible foundationalism.** We first see the stones orbiting Simone, with beliefs floating above them.
4. **The regress.** The legend of YYYY SOWATT begins with an archive keeper who says someone wrote a line that night. The four Y voices question the wet ink, its drying time, and the keeper’s earlier test. SOWHAT asks how the keeper knows the ink hasn’t changed. The flashback moves through skepticism, infinitism, and coherentism before Descartes finds a starting point in the act of thinking. [Regress illustration](../public/generated/backgrounds/dead-ink/regress-problem.webp) · [Design decision](design-decisions.md#2026-11-21t193341-0800--keep-the-regress-legend)

5. **The dilemma.** The Cartesian detective won't name a suspect until it's beyond *all possible* doubt, so the case stays open forever. The moderate detective stops at what she saw, but why stop *there*?
6. **Undoubted, not undoubtable.** The stones in play: Evan wrote the card, the card says eight, Simone read it correctly, Evan is dead. Each *could* be doubted. Nobody here is doubting them.
7. **The three routes.** Each way of making stones gets a quick demonstration on the platform: social (Cole's chat), hinges (asking a question conjures stones), knowledge (Simone sees the departure board). **Belief at the base** is a short gag.
8. **The crew** [open]. Somewhere in Part 1, a heist-movie lineup introduces the investigators by role: subject, speaker, assessor, and the one who only expresses. Where it goes is still to decide.

**Quizzes:** the regress exit (Part 1, item 4) and the hinge (item 7).

## Part 2 · The Context Line (routes × versions)

Rae punches the first ticket, and the train starts hopping universes. Every stop replays 7:12 to 8:40 under that universe's stone physics. **Not every universe solves the case.** Some reach confident, justified, wrong answers.

| Stop (route × version) | What happens | Teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social × SI | Simone's own community, Evan's friends, lets "Milo was alone with Evan" pass, so a stone forms and Simone accuses Milo. Cole's chat cheering doesn't count: "Your chat isn't my community." | It's the subject's community that matters, not the speaker's |
| Social × contextualism | Cole says "Simone is justified in accusing Milo" to his chill chat and then to the pedants. Different stones light up for each, so the sentence makes two claims. The Retrograde raid forms a stone that shouldn't count. | The speaker's audience fixes the claim; consensus can be wrong |
| Hinges × SI | Simone's inquiry shifts to "Was the card even Evan's?" The stone "Evan wrote the card" dissolves for her and becomes the question. | The inquiry decides what is a hinge |
| Hinges × contextualism | Cole's stream segments ("timeline" versus "was it staged?") make the same line say different things. | The speaker's inquiry fixes the claim |
| Knowledge × relativism | Rae looks at the evidence stones of the investigators accusing *her* and sees them hollow. To everyone else they're solid. NOT VALID. | Truth relative to the assessor |
| Knowledge × expressivism | No stones exist. Echo tucks his claw at every accusation. He never once commits to "Evan is dead." | Calling something foundational is a commitment, not a description |
| Belief at the base (short gag) | Every passing suspicion becomes a stone, and everyone is arrested at once. | Harman's general foundationalism |

**Climax of Part 2: disagreement.** The investigators compare notes in the dining car. Cole: "I'm starving." Rae: "I'm not." No conflict. Then Cole: "Simone is justified in accusing Milo." Rae punches: **"Not valid."** *That* feels like real disagreement. The exits are relativism, expressivism (claw against punch), or "overstated." **Quiz:** whose context matters in each universe?

## Part 3 · The last word (access internalism, the supervenience base)

Back in the home universe at 8:40. The case now turns on **the time**.

1. **Access.** Cole asks, "Are you justified in *thinking* you're justified?" Simone looks for her own stone "the card says 8:00" and finds it flickering. She can't tell whether it's there. Her higher-order doubt leaks down into doubt about the time.
2. **The supervenience base.** Normally, what Evan wrote fixes what she should believe about the time, and she can read what he wrote. The stone is local, and she can see it. A wrong forecast on the hallway TV reminds us why a *global* home is hopeless.
3. **The base moves.** The stone "Evan wrote 8:00" dissolves, because the hour is unreadable. New stones condense from things Simone *can* know: Evan's own boast that his ink dries in ten minutes; the smear was still wet when guests found him at 8:40; Cole's stream timestamps. So Evan was writing at about **8:30**, not 8:00. Simone can see each new stone. She can tell what she's justified in believing, even though the original evidence is gone. **Quiz:** what fixes Simone's justification now?
4. **The hinge breaks.** The 8:30 timeline clears every suspect, because none of them was near the desk then. And a wet line that *thins out* mid-stroke isn't a struggle. It's a pen running dry. Simone asks the one question nobody asked: "Is he actually dead?" The stone "Evan is dead," present since the case opened, cracks and dissolves on screen.
5. **Ending.** Simone refills Evan. He gasps, bows, and writes a fresh **8:30**. "You're early." The guests shout "SURPRISE!" Echo clacks once; he never raised his claw for the murder. Rae punches Simone's card: **"Valid."** The corner of her mouth lifts for one frame.

## Why this structure works

- **The three parts map exactly onto the canvas:** Part 1 = *Why accept flexible foundationalism?*, Part 2 = *Which version?*, Part 3 = *What does this explain?*
- **The universes make the pairings memorable.** Each pairing becomes a place with its own rules instead of an abstract table cell.
- **The stones make abstract foundations visible.** Their formation rules *are* the three routes, and whether you can see your own stones *is* the access question.
- **The twist is philosophy, not just plot.** The murder's hidden hinge ("Evan is dead") is Wittgenstein's point acted out, and the solved time is Greco's mobile cognitive home acted out.
- **The characters stay coherent.** Each one leads the universe that matches their temperament.

## Open questions

1. **Victim and solution.** Is "no murder, he ran dry" too much of a cheat for a murder-mystery hook? Alternatives with a real culprit:
   - **(a) Cole, accidentally.** His selfie stick knocked Evan off the desk at 8:31. The stream timestamp plus the ink's drying time convict him.
   - **(b) Echo.** He pinched Evan to "endorse" the invitation, and the claw snapped the nib. His expressivist silence becomes a confession.
   - **(c) A real villain** among the NPCs (the Timetable Society?), with the time as the alibi-breaker.
2. **"Self-justification."** Does this mean the *social* route, or Harman's *belief at the base* (every belief foundational)? The draft includes both.
3. **The character lineup.** Where should the investigators be introduced: at the start, at the end of Part 1, or at the start of Part 2?
4. **Stone visibility.** Can characters see stones (part of the world, as proposed), or should the stones be an audience-only overlay?
5. **How many universes.** Seven stops is a lot for about 28 minutes. Which four or five matter most? Proposed core: social × SI, social × contextualism, hinges × SI, knowledge × relativism, knowledge × expressivism.
6. **Tone.** "Murder" over a fountain pen keeps it dry and silly. Is that the register you want, or something closer to real noir?
7. **The OP.** Once the story is settled, the opening should flash the case (the "8:" card), the stones, the train between universes and the four investigators. The OP work is paused until then.
