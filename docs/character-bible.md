# PHI 102 canvas · Character bible: personality, acting and expression

**Canonical for how the cast thinks, feels, moves and speaks. November 21, 2026.** Identity details (face shape, hair, costume, colour, props) come from the [Version C model sheets](character-references/version-c/) and the [story bible](story-and-characters.md). This file covers acting: expression, posture, gesture, motion and dialogue tone. Read it before writing any image, video or dialogue prompt that shows a member of the cast.

## Why this file exists

In recent generations, faces and body language drifted away from who the characters are. Three causes are visible in the frames:

1. **The prompts gave each person a single adjective.** Simone was "practical and deadpan," Rae was "imperturbable," and Cole was "an animated livestreamer." Image models tend to render *deadpan* as sulky and *imperturbable* as hostile.
2. **The attached model sheets give the model its strongest cue for acting.** In the bottom rows of the JUSTINE and JO sheets, Simone glares at her watch, pouts, and slumps bored with her chin in her hand, while Rae scowls with pursed lips. The model copies those faces. In [frame 4](story-illustrations/04-cole-makes-the-claim.png), Simone gives a chin-on-hand side-eye and Rae sneers with a hand on her hip. In [frame 5](story-illustrations/05-punch-and-claw.png), Rae has slanted brows and a pout, and Simone slumps with her chin in her hand again.
3. **Nothing in the prompts said how the quiet characters differ.** Simone and Rae are both calm, so they collapsed into the same irritated face.

The fix has three parts: written acting notes for every shot, rules for which sheet panels may be used, and new acting sheets for Simone and Rae (prompts at the end).

## How an agent or model should use this file

1. Read the beat in [story-and-characters.md](story-and-characters.md).
2. Read the section below for each character in the shot, plus that beat's row in the [acting map](#beat-by-beat-acting-map).
3. Attach model sheets **for identity only** and follow the [reference-image rules](#reference-image-rules).
4. Build the prompt with the [template](#prompt-template). Paste each character's prompt block and add one line about what that character feels at this moment.
5. Check the result against the [review checklist](#review-checklist) before showing it.

When this file conflicts with an older prompt in the storyboard README, this file wins.

**Contrast rule.** Whenever two or more of them share a shot, their energies must read differently at thumbnail size. *Cole performs, Simone notices, Rae assesses, Echo abides.* If two faces look alike, the shot has failed.

**Classic roles, for direction only.** Simone is the straight man who is also the heart of the story, a quiet, capable heroine whose feelings are read in close-up. Cole is the chorus and the sports commentator who narrates events as they happen. Rae is the stone-faced official, playing the silent-film comedy of total composure, and the gatekeeper. Echo is the small, silent creature who seems to know more than anyone. Keep film, actor and character names out of image prompts, because models copy likenesses.

## Simone — the subject (sensitive invariantism)

**Logline.** The calm one who is actually living through the problem everyone else is discussing.

**Who she is.** Composed, observant, conscientious and quietly caring. She feels everything a little and shows it a little. She is over-prepared because she cares: it's Echo's birthday and he's in her keeping. Her dry humour comes from understatement, not irritation. She is fond of Echo, and he is the only one she openly softens for.

- **Wants:** to get Echo to his party on time.
- **Fears:** being the reason he misses it.
- **Under pressure:** she gets quieter and more precise, never louder.

**Philosophy into acting.** On sensitive invariantism, whether *her* belief is justified depends on *her own* situation. So Simone's face is where changes in her situation show. When her evidence shifts (the prank mention, the smudge, the doubt about her reading), a small change appears on her face first. Everyone else talks *about* her. She quietly updates.

**Face, at rest.** Brows level and relaxed. Upper lids slightly lowered: calm, but not sleepy and not narrowed. Clear, attentive eyes with a catchlight. Mouth closed and soft, neutral or with the faintest hint of a smile. Head tilted slightly when she listens.

**Body.** Upright with relaxed shoulders. Economical gestures. One hand often rests near the carrier strap, her watch or the invitation. She stands with her weight settled and walks briskly, with purpose.

**Emotion dial** (0 at rest, 3 at most; she rarely goes past 2):

| Level | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 0 · Composed | As above. Present and alert. |
| 1 · Noticing | Eyes open a fraction wider, a slow blink, a quick glance down at the watch, card or Echo, lips pressed lightly together. |
| 2 · Moved | *Worry:* inner brows lift slightly, lips part a little, her hand tightens on the strap. *Warmth:* a small closed-mouth smile, eyes softening toward Echo. *Amusement:* one corner of the mouth lifts, a short exhale through the nose. *Doubt:* a faint crease between the brows, eyes narrowed to *study* (not out of hostility), fingertip to lip while she is actively reading. |
| 3 · Peak (once, at the ending) | Stillness, eyes wide, lips slightly parted. Then a quiet exhale and a small, rueful smile. No shouting. |

**Signature tells.** Checks her watch with a quick, practised turn of the wrist. Glances at Echo before a decision. Squares the edges of the invitation. Takes a small breath before she moves.

**Comedy.** She plays it straight. The joke is her composure against Cole's noise. When Cole goes too far, she gives him a level look: patient, mildly amused, waiting. It is not a glare.

**Never.** A glaring side-eye, a sulky pout, a sneer, an eye-roll, a bored slump with her chin in her hand, crossed arms with attitude, an open-mouthed shout, tears, panic or flirtation.

**On video.** Small, continuous life: breathing, blinking, glances that land and hold for half a second. A change of feeling takes about a second and starts in the eyes. Her hands are decisive.

**Dialogue.** Short, polite and exact, delivered evenly. "Can we talk about this on the train?"

**Image prompt block**

```text
Simone: calm, observant and quietly caring. Relaxed level brows, clear attentive eyes with a catchlight, soft closed mouth. Her feeling shows only in a small way: [THIS SHOT, e.g. "a faint crease of concern between the brows" / "a small fond smile toward Echo"]. Upright, relaxed shoulders, economical gestures. Not sulky, not glaring, not bored, no pout, no chin-on-hand slump.
```

**Video motion block**

```text
Simone stays composed and quietly alive: steady breathing, natural blinks, short glances that land and hold. [THIS SHOT'S CHANGE, e.g. "her eyes widen slightly and she exhales"] unfolds slowly over about a second, starting in the eyes. Minimal, decisive hand movement. No eye-rolls, no slumping.
```

## Cole — the speaker (contextualism)

**Logline.** The live narrator who can't experience anything without describing it to an audience.

**Who he is.** Warm, sociable, generous and a bit much. He genuinely likes Simone and wants her to make it. He is her hype man, not her antagonist. He is confident without malice and commits completely to every bit.

- **Wants:** to be in the moment, preferably on stream.
- **Fears:** dead air.
- **Under pressure:** he talks faster and turns to the camera.

**Philosophy into acting.** On contextualism, the same sentence expresses different propositions depending on the speaker's conversational context. Cole's defining trait, then, is that **he switches register to suit his audience.** He reads his chat and changes mode at once:

- **Group-chat mode** (an easygoing audience): loose and grinning, a wink, leaning back, a casual hand wave.
- **"For the record" mode** (pedants in the stream chat): he snaps upright, puts on a serious news-anchor face with raised brows, holds a flat palm out, and holds the phone level like a newscaster.

Show the switch as a visible snap. It is both the gag and the philosophy.

**His chat is the objector-group.** Cole never commits to a claim until he has glanced at his chat. A 👍 from the chill chat means "settled, move on." A "source??" from the pedants means he backs up and adds a reason. A raid from the Retrograde Car shows him (and us) that an audience can be wrong. In shots, show this as a quick glance at the phone *before* he speaks, then the register snap. See [the stream audience as objector-group](story-v2-draft.md#the-stream-audience-is-the-objector-group).

**Face, at rest.** A big, open grin showing teeth, bright eyes, raised brows. His eyes keep going to the phone lens or the audience.

**Body.** Always moving. He leans into the camera and makes big open-handed gestures. The selfie stick works like an extension of his arm. The ring light is always on.

**Emotion dial**

| Level | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 1 · Hype | Grin, wink, pointing at the lens, bouncing on his heels. |
| 2 · Performed surprise or outrage | "Oh, come on." Palm out, eyebrows up, mouth open, half-turned to the camera. |
| 3 · Genuine shock or pain | Wide eyes and an open mouth, recoiling. For example, "Ow, that hurts" when Echo pinches him: comic exaggeration, never real distress. |

**Signature tells.** He talks into the phone even when addressing the people beside him. He winks at the lens. The earbud wire is always visible. He reads the chat out loud.

**Never.** A smug villain's smirk, sly narrowed scheming eyes, a sneer, cruel mockery, sullenness, boredom or cool detachment. His brows are angular by design, so the prompt has to make his expression read *friendly*.

**On video.** Constant motion and quick gesture beats. He turns the camera on himself and then on others. He switches register in a single beat.

**Dialogue.** Casual, punchy, with emoji energy. In formal mode he uses mock-official phrasing: "For the record, chat…"

**Image prompt block**

```text
Cole: warm, sunny livestream hype man, fully committed to the bit. Big open friendly grin, bright eyes, raised brows, big open-handed gesture, leaning toward his phone lens. [THIS SHOT, e.g. "mid-switch into serious newscaster mode, sitting bolt upright"]. Friendly and sincere, never smug, sly or sneering.
```

**Video motion block**

```text
Cole never stops moving: quick gestures, head bobs, leaning in and out of his phone's ring light, glancing at the live chat. [THIS SHOT'S CHANGE, e.g. "he snaps from a loose grin to a mock-serious anchor face in one beat"]. Energetic and friendly, never mean.
```

## Rae — the assessor (relativism)

**Logline.** The conductor who rules on everything and never shows you what she feels.

**Who she is.** Unhurried, exact and self-possessed. She has punched a million tickets and seen every kind of passenger. Nothing surprises her. Her authority comes from total composure. She is cool, not cold-hearted: if anything moves her, she keeps it to herself.

- **Wants:** to do the job correctly by her standards.
- **Fears:** nothing she will let you see.
- **Under pressure:** there is no visible change. She simply takes a beat longer.

**Story link (draft 2).** Rae is a regular guest at Evan's parties and knows he folds his cards while the ink is wet. She never volunteers this. She rules; she doesn't advise. Her silence is part of her character, not a plot convenience.

**Philosophy into acting.** On relativism, whether Cole's claim is true depends on the context of whoever assesses it, and no assessment context is privileged. So Rae never argues, never tries to persuade, and never needs anyone to agree. She delivers her verdict without heat because it is valid *by her standards*, full stop. **That is why she is cool, not angry.** Anger would imply a fight. She is just stamping a ticket.

**Face, at rest.** Neutral and level. Brows straight and **horizontal**, not slanting down. Upper lids lowered halfway for a cool, level gaze. Steady, direct eyes. Mouth a closed, **straight line**, with a relaxed jaw. Her face is nearly symmetrical. It should read as *unreadable*, not *annoyed*.

**Body.** Upright, very still, weight centred, shoulders square. She moves only when she means to. The punch pliers hang loosely in one hand, and she holds a ticket precisely between two fingers. She stands in the aisle as if she owns it, without hand-on-hip attitude.

**Emotion dial.** Her feeling lives in timing and action, not in her face.

| Level | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 0 · Unreadable | As above. |
| 1 · Assessing | A slow look down at the ticket or the speaker. A pause. |
| 2 · Verdict | One crisp *click* of the punch. The punch is the expression, and her face doesn't change. At most, one eyebrow lifts a hair. |
| 3 · Hairline crack (once, at 8:30) | After punching "Valid," the faintest lift at one corner of the mouth for a single frame. |

**Signature tells.** She pauses before she speaks. She punches tickets with a precise click. She adjusts her cap brim with two fingers. She eats her sandwich calmly ("I'm not hungry"). She reads tickets at arm's length.

**Never.** A scowl, angry slanted brows, pursed or pouting lips, a sneer, a smirk, an eye-roll, a sassy hand on the hip, theatrical chin-stroking scepticism, a grin, laughter, shouting, fluster or surprise.

**On video.** Minimal motion and one action per beat. Her head turns in one smooth movement and then stops. She blinks rarely. For the punch: hold, click, hold. Let the stillness be funny.

**Dialogue.** Few words, delivered flat and even. "Not valid." "What makes you think that?" "I'm not hungry."

**Image prompt block**

```text
Rae: cool, unreadable and completely composed. Brows straight and level, a calm half-lidded level gaze, mouth a closed straight neutral line, relaxed jaw, nearly symmetrical face. Upright, still, weight centred. [THIS SHOT, e.g. "she punches the ticket with no change of expression"]. Her emotion never reaches her face: no scowl, no slanted angry brows, no pursed or pouting lips, no smirk, no hand-on-hip attitude.
```

**Video motion block**

```text
Rae is almost perfectly still. She makes one deliberate action at a time: [THIS SHOT, e.g. "she looks down at the ticket, pauses, punches it with a single crisp click, and holds"]. Her head turns in one smooth movement and stops. She rarely blinks. Her face stays neutral and level throughout.
```

### Telling Simone and Rae apart

Both are quiet. **Simone is calm and open:** her feelings sit close to the surface, her eyes are soft and alive, and she reacts. **Rae is calm and closed:** her eyes are level and steady, and she doesn't react. She rules. In a shared frame, give Simone a catchlight and a slight tilt of the head, and give Rae a level gaze and a square posture.

## Echo — the hermit crab (expressivism)

**Logline.** The guest of honour who expresses and never describes.

**Who he is.** Unbothered, dignified and patient, like an elder at his own birthday party. ("It's a surprise. Echo knows.")

**Philosophy into acting.** On expressivism, calling a belief "foundationally justified" expresses a commitment rather than describing a fact. So Echo has exactly **one expressive channel: the claw.** Claw raised (*clack*) means endorsement: "I'm treating this as a starting point." Claw tucked means withholding. His face never changes.

**Face.** Two ivory eyes on stalks, always half-lidded, and a small flat line for a mouth. Nothing else.

**Body.** Still. The claw is binary and crisp. His eye stalks may swivel slowly to look at something.

**Never.** A smile, an open mouth, wide round eyes, fear, waving both claws, dancing, cute or chibi styling, or a second crab in the same shot.

**Image prompt block**

```text
Echo: one palm-sized dignified, unbothered hermit crab. Half-lidded ivory eye stalks, flat expression that never changes. His one oversized coral claw is [RAISED high in a single crisp endorsement / TUCKED in, withholding]. No smile, no wide eyes, no cute styling.
```

**Video motion block**

```text
Echo stays motionless for long stretches. His eye stalks swivel slowly. [Claw: "he raises his one big claw in a single clean snap and holds it" / "his claw stays tucked"]. His face never changes.
```

## Ensemble chemistry

- **Cole and Simone:** he narrates, she lives it. She gives him patient, level looks, and he is genuinely rooting for her.
- **Cole and Rae:** motion against stillness. He escalates, and she doesn't move. The joke works only if she stays still.
- **Rae and Echo:** two still characters with different kinds of stillness. Rae's is authority: upright, with a level gaze. Echo's is indifference: half-lidded and settled into his shell. Their disagreement is punch against claw, so it plays out in actions, not faces.
- **Simone and Echo:** the story's only open tenderness. Her face softens when she looks at him.

## Prompt template

Keep the shared art-direction block from the [storyboard prompts](story-illustrations/README.md). Then assemble each request in this order:

1. **Art direction** (the existing shared block).
2. **Reference rule:** "Use the attached sheets for identity only: face shape, hair, costume, colours and props. Take expressions and body language from the written acting notes, not from the sheets."
3. **Identity line** for each character, using the descriptions in the existing prompts.
4. **Acting block** for each character, copied from this file, with the shot-specific bracket filled in.
5. **Scene**, action and camera.
6. **Contrast line:** "Each person's emotional energy must read differently at a glance: Cole performs, Simone notices, Rae assesses, Echo abides."

Put the acting blocks *before* the scene description. Models give more weight to cues that appear early in a prompt.

**Worked example: the NOT VALID close-up**

```text
Acting:
Rae: cool, unreadable and completely composed. Brows straight and level, a calm half-lidded level gaze, mouth a closed straight neutral line, relaxed jaw. She squeezes the punch onto the ticket with no change of expression; the punch is her whole verdict. No scowl, no slanted angry brows, no pursed lips, no smirk.
Echo: one palm-sized dignified, unbothered hermit crab, half-lidded ivory eye stalks, flat expression. His one oversized coral claw is raised high in a single crisp endorsement.
Simone, at the edge: calm and attentive, watching the standoff with a quick glance toward Echo; relaxed level brows, soft closed mouth. Not slumped, not chin-on-hand, not glaring.
Cole, at the edge: delighted, big friendly grin, filming the standoff. Friendly, not sly.
Each person's emotional energy must read differently at a glance.
```

## Review checklist

Check every generated frame or clip before showing it:

- [ ] At thumbnail size, can you tell the temperaments apart? (Cole performs, Simone notices, Rae assesses, Echo abides.)
- [ ] **Simone:** her eyes are alive and the feeling is small. There is no sulk, glare, pout or chin-on-hand slump.
- [ ] **Rae:** level brows and a straight mouth. No scowl, pout, smirk or hand on the hip. Any verdict is expressed through the punch.
- [ ] **Cole:** big energy that reads as friendly, not sly.
- [ ] **Echo:** half-lidded, one crab, one clear claw state.
- [ ] No face copies an off-tone sheet panel.
- [ ] Each character's feeling matches the beat in the acting map.
