# PHI 102 canvas · Story draft 4: *Until It Dries*

**Concept draft for discussion, November 21, 2026.** This replaces the party, the train and the murder from drafts 2 and 3 with a new plot. The character designs ([character bible](character-bible.md)) and YYYY SOWATT stay fixed. The world keeps one rule from the wet-ink legend: **ink dries only when nobody is asking about it.** Drafts 2 ([*Eight-ish*](story-v2-draft.md)) and 3 ([*Dead Ink*](story-v3-dead-ink.md)) stay on file. Bracketed questions mark what we still need to decide.

## Logline

YYYY SOWATT has escaped, and the city, built from dried sentences, is starting to melt. Simone, Cole, Rae and Echo split across four drafts of reality, each with its own rule for when ink dries. They're looking for a word strong enough to seal the creature away again. What they find instead is that sealing it was never the answer.

## The world

### Wet ink and dry ink

Everything in this world is written. A sentence stays **wet** while anyone is asking about it. Left alone, it dries. "Dry" means "settled."

### Ink sticks (replacing the foundation stones)

A sentence that nobody is asking about dries into an **ink stick**: a solid bar of dried ink with the claim pressed into its face, like the embossed ink sticks used in calligraphy. Ink sticks are the story's foundations.

| Rule | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody is asking, so it dries | The wet sentence hardens into a stick |
| A question is water | Ask about a stick seriously and it softens back into wet ink |
| Undoubted, not undoubtable | A stick is solid, but any stick melts if you pour water on it |
| Foundations support other beliefs | You grind a stick into fresh ink to write new conclusions |
| Building on foundations | The city's streets and bridges are built from sticks like *This bridge holds* |

The last row gives the story its stakes. When questions come back, the ground literally softens.

### YYYY SOWATT

Long ago, someone spilled a question mark into the world's great inkwell. It never dried. It grew. YYYY SOWATT is a chorus of five ink bubbles: four Y voices, whose lines always start with "Why," and SOWHAT, the smallest, marked with a red dot like a teacher's red-pen "So what?" in the margin. The Y voices ask what supports a reason. SOWHAT asks whether the reason supports the claim at all. Write a sentence near it and it rises out of your wet ink. As long as it keeps asking, the ink can't dry.

### Drafts (the parallel universes)

The parallel universes are **drafts** of the same page. Each draft has its own ink physics: a different rule for whose questions keep ink wet. Rae is the conductor between drafts. Her punch pliers punch a hole through the page, and you step through into the next draft.

## Act 1 · The Thaw (*Why accept flexible foundationalism?*)

1. **Cold open.** On a stream, Cole crosses a bridge carved with *This bridge holds.* Five bubbles drift up: "Why?" The sentence goes wet and the bridge slumps into the river. Simone grabs Echo. They're cut off from home. In the background, a claw-shaped crack runs across the broken seal of the Great Inkwell.

2. **The regress (legend).**

   > **Narrator:** Long ago, every word in the world was written in ink. Left alone, ink dries in minutes. But ink can't dry while someone is still asking about it.
   > **Narrator:** Then someone spilled a question mark into the great inkwell. People called what grew there YYYY SOWATT.
   >
   > **Pen** *(writing)***:** "The bridge will hold."
   > **Y:** Why believe it will hold?
   > **Pen:** I crossed it this morning.
   > **Y:** Why think this morning tells you about tonight?
   > **Pen:** Bridges don't change overnight.
   > **Y:** Why believe they don't?
   > **Pen:** This one never has.
   > **Y:** Why trust "never has"?
   > **Pen:** I've crossed it every day for forty years.
   > **SOWHAT:** So what? Every bridge holds until the day it doesn't.
   >
   > **Narrator:** Her words never dried. Neither did the bridge.

   **The four exits**, each as a picture:

   | Exit | In the ink world |
   | --- | --- |
   | Skepticism | Some people stopped writing altogether. Oddly, they were the calmest people around (the Pyrrhonists sought tranquility by suspending judgment). |
   | Infinitism | Some kept answering. Their scrolls rolled over the hills, still wet the whole way down. |
   | Coherentism | Some pinned their letters in a ring, each one vouching for the next. YYYY SOWATT just swam in circles. |
   | Foundationalism | Legend says Descartes, shut in a stove-heated room in the winter of 1619, found the one stick no question could soften: *I think.* He used it to seal YYYY SOWATT in the Great Inkwell. |

   Keep the narrator's "Legend says…": Descartes wasn't really the first foundationalist (Aristotle had first principles, and Unit 1.1 covers Agrippa).

3. **The dilemma.** The four go to the Great Inkwell to reseal it. Descartes's stick is intact, and it's the size of a grain of rice. "I think" can't tell you whether a bridge holds. That's the **Cartesian** side of the dilemma: true starting points, but far too few. Cole tries "But I can *see* the street's solid!" SOWATT asks, "Why stop there?" and the stick melts. That's the **moderate** side, in Greco's own terms: once the doubt is in play, how much help is "it looks solid"?

4. **Twist 1: the city was never built on Descartes.** The seal didn't hold anything up. It only kept the questions out. Every street was undoubted, not undoubtable. The goal changes: they don't need words nobody *can* question. They need places where the question isn't being asked.

5. **Three ways to keep a question out (the three routes).**
   - **Social activity:** your audience wouldn't ask it.
   - **Hinges:** asking one question holds others dry. Even SOWATT's questions need dry words to stand on: you can't ask "Why believe the bridge holds?" without taking "bridge" for granted. (Wittgenstein's point that doubt itself depends on certainty.)
   - **Knowledge at the base:** what you see with your own eyes dries instantly.

   [Where does **belief at the base** (Harman) go? A short gag: a draft where every passing thought dries on the spot.]

**Quizzes:** the regress exit (item 2) and the hinge (item 5).

## Act 2 · The Drafts (*Which version?*)

Rae punches four holes. Each character takes a draft that matches their temperament.

| Draft | Ink physics | Quest | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simone** (sensitive invariantism × hinges) | Whether ink dries depends on *her* situation | Find a dry path home | While she's asking "Which way home?", the street stays dry under her. When she starts asking "Is this even my street?", it softens. Her inquiry decides. |
| **Cole** (contextualism × social) | Ink dries on *words*, depending on the speaker's audience | Broadcast which bridges are safe | "This bridge holds" is dry for his chill chat and wet for the pedants (the Timetable Society). Same sentence, two claims. Then a horoscope raid dries a sentence that shouldn't be dry. |
| **Rae** (relativism) | A stick looks dry or wet depending on *who's looking* | Certify the bridges | She looks at Cole's bridge and punches: "Not valid." |
| **Echo** (expressivism) | No sticks at all, only claws | Never stated | Calling a stick dry *is* raising a claw. |

**Climax: disagreement.** Cole: "This bridge holds." Rae: *click*, "Not valid." Echo's claw goes up. It feels like a real disagreement, which is exactly the problem relativism and expressivism try to explain. Greco leaves open which works best. **Quiz:** whose context matters in each draft?

**Twist 2: the Dry Draft.** Looking for a way to destroy SOWATT, they punch into a draft where someone already did. Everything there is dry forever. It's gleaming and permanent, and wrong. The citizens (the Retrograde crowd) stand on sticks like "Mercury retrograde weakens bridges," and they can never fix anything. That's Greco's horoscope worry: a whole community can take something for granted and still be wrong. **A world with no questions isn't safe. It's stuck.** So destroying SOWATT was the wrong goal.

## Act 3 · Getting Home (*What does this explain?*)

1. **Access.** As the water rises, Cole asks Simone, "Are you justified in thinking you're justified?" The stick under her feet softens. Her doubt about her own grounds literally melts the ground she's standing on: Greco's point that doubt about your grounds carries down to the belief itself.
2. **The supervenience base.** Her map said "Home is across the Red Bridge." The map's words fixed her grounds, and she could read them. Now the map has run. If her grounds still depended on words she can't read, she'd have no way to tell what she should believe. [Optional: a confidently wrong weather forecast on a melting billboard shows why a *global* cognitive home is hopeless.]
3. **The mobile home.** Echo climbs out of his shell and into a new one. A hermit crab is literally an animal with a mobile home, and Greco's term is "mobile home." Simone follows his lead and switches to facts she can know right now: the sound of the river, Cole's live view, her own footsteps. Dry ground forms under her one step at a time. **Quiz:** what fixes Simone's justification now?
4. **Twist 3: who broke the seal?** Echo did. The bridge in the cold open was rotting, but *This bridge holds* had dried long ago, and nobody could question it anymore. The city was turning into the Dry Draft. Echo can't argue, because he never describes anything. So he acted: he let the questions back in. The bridge would have fallen anyway, with people on it.
5. **Ending.** SOWATT shrinks down to its smallest voice. SOWHAT sits on Simone's shoulder, a question that shows up only when it matters. Rae punches Simone's ticket: "Valid." The corner of her mouth lifts for one frame.

## Twists and their clues

| Twist | Clues planted earlier |
| --- | --- |
| 1. The city never stood on Descartes's stick | The stick is the size of a grain of rice; streets were melting only where someone asked |
| 2. A world without SOWATT is stuck, not safe | Cole's horoscope raid dries a false sentence in Act 2 |
| 3. Echo broke the seal, to save the city | The claw-shaped crack in the cold open; Echo is the only one who never raises his claw for *This bridge holds*; his draft has no sticks, so SOWATT can't touch him; the legend's last line, "Every bridge holds until the day it doesn't" |

## Why this structure works

- **The three acts map onto the canvas:** Act 1 = *Why accept flexible foundationalism?*, Act 2 = *Which version?*, Act 3 = *What does this explain?*
- **One rule carries the paper.** "Ink dries when nobody is asking" is flexible foundationalism. Descartes's stick shows the Cartesian standard, and the Dry Draft shows why consensus can still be wrong.
- **Every twist is philosophy.** Twist 1 is *undoubted, not undoubtable*. Twist 2 is the horoscope worry. Twist 3 turns the expressivist's silence into action.
- **The characters stay coherent.** Each leads the draft that matches their temperament, and Echo's hermit-crab shell becomes the mobile home.

## Open questions

1. **Twist 3's visibility.** Hidden until the end, or hinted strongly enough that sharp viewers guess it in Act 2?
2. **The legend chain.** The bridge chain leans on inductive skepticism (Unit 3). It ties to the class but slightly blurs the regress with Hume's problem. Alternatives are below.
3. **Length.** Three acts, four drafts and three twists is a lot for about 28 minutes. Keep each draft to one beat and one image.
4. **Knowledge route placement.** Act 1 demo only, or also a draft beat?
5. **Lichtenberg's objection.** Should SOWHAT show that even *I think* can be questioned ("Who said there's an 'I'?"), to make *undoubted, not undoubtable* sharper?

## Alternative legend chains (earlier drafts)

**Snow (ends on dream doubt, a direct bridge to Descartes's Meditation I):**

> **Scribe** *(writing)***:** "It snowed last night."
> **Y:** Why do you believe it snowed? — Look outside. The whole street is white.
> **Y:** Why think white means snow? — It's freezing, and it crunches under my boots.
> **Y:** Why trust what you feel? — I'm wide awake, and my senses work when I'm awake.
> **Y:** Why believe you're awake? — I pinched myself. It hurt.
> **SOWHAT:** So what? Pinches hurt in dreams too.

**Party (mirrors Greco's Peter-and-Justine example; written for the draft 3 plot):**

> **Pen** *(writing)***:** "The party starts at eight."
> **Y:** Why believe it starts at eight? — The host told me himself.
> **Y:** Why trust the host? — He has never once given me the wrong time.
> **Y:** Why think you heard him right? — He said it right in my ear, clear as a bell.
> **Y:** Why trust your memory of that? — I wrote it down the moment he said it.
> **SOWHAT:** So what? You'd copy a mistake just as neatly.
> **Pen:** …Oh, come on.
> **The four Ys:** Why?
> **Narrator:** She was too polite to stop answering. Her invitation never dried. She did.
